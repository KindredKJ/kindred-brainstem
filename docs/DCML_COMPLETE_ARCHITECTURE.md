# Complete DCML learning-loop architecture

BRAINSTEM is the cognitive model. DCML is native inside BRAINSTEM. The loopback runtime serves BRAINSTEM and delegates typed calls; attached models remain subordinate instruments.

The implemented local loop is: typed experience → evidence verification → deterministic outcome evaluation → non-uniform credit assignment → signed founder learning approval → immutable dataset → numerical policy optimization → candidate checkpoint → held-out canary → signed promotion → changed policy selection → benchmark/replay/consolidation or signed rollback. Foundation-model and adapter weights remain `NOT_TRAINED`.

SQLite migration 2 adds append-only, hash-addressed records for verification, rich experiences, evaluations, credit, datasets, replay, curriculum, concepts, causal and counterfactual records, performance, calibration, metacognition, policy parameters, training, canaries, benchmarks, regressions, signed approvals, lineage, consolidation, and missions without altering v1 records.

Additive migration 4 supplies advanced intervention, transfer, routing, temporal-credit, skill, counterfactual-observation, and benchmark records without modifying legacy state.

Additive migration 5 supplies unique decision nonces and durable idempotent submission claims without rewriting legacy tables. Version 1 decisions intentionally fail closed because they do not carry the environment, tenant, approver-key identity, mandatory expiry, and replay bindings required for execution.

The advanced loop forms concepts from structural features across language, source, structured records, mission outcomes, and instrument records. Transfer requires structural applicability above a configured threshold; matching words alone are explicitly insufficient. Causal hypotheses remain hypotheses until interventions alter confidence, preserve confounders and alternatives, and may falsify them. Counterfactual regret and metacognitive findings create governed proposals, never direct production mutations.

Attached-model routing considers capability, health, privacy, risk, cost, latency, historical success, and calibration. An unavailable chosen route is recorded and fails closed; no implicit fallback is performed. The active learned policy remains owned by BRAINSTEM and is restored from its checkpoint after restart.
